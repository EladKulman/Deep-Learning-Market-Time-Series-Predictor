# Independent review of the results (re-derived from `docs/results/`)

All numbers below were recomputed from the raw CSVs with `review/part1.py`, `part2.py` (outputs `part1.out`, `part2*.out`, `part3.out` in the same scratch dir). Paths are relative to the repo root. "WQL" deltas are per-window pinball normalised by the fold's sum|y| (so window means equal aggregate WQL deltas); intervals are 5,000-draw paired bootstraps over windows.

## 1. Headline claims, checked

**(a) Zero-shot ladder (`docs/results/pretrained_ladder/<fold>/*_predictions.csv`).** Rungs r1–r7 reproduce exactly: pooled r7_all_external +0.0045 [+0.0004, +0.0088] (worse), r3_control_nofuture −0.0015 [−0.0030, −0.0000], r5_plus_fed −0.0009 [−0.0019, +0.0002], r2 −0.0020 [−0.0045, +0.0005]. Per-fold significant cells: r7 2023 +0.0120, nofuture 2025H2 −0.0043, fed 2025H2 −0.0031 — as documented.

**One row is wrong.** The doc's r0_target line (+0.0047 pooled, +0.0211 in 2025, "the control helps, mostly via 2025") is an artefact. `r0_target_predictions.csv` holds 500 rows: 250 `r0_target` and 250 `gaussian` under the same file, and the paired-delta step did not filter by `model`. The doc's r0 numbers are exactly the average of the true r0 and Gaussian deltas (2025: (+0.0040 + 0.0383)/2 = +0.0211; 2022: (−0.0035 − 0.0093)/2 = −0.0064). True r0 vs control: **−0.0001 [−0.0037, +0.0034] pooled**, +0.0040 in 2025. The walk-forward pretrained arm confirms it (`walk_forward/wql_pretrained.csv`: r0 mean 0.6977 vs r3 0.6975). So the 21-feature control adds nothing zero-shot; the doc's sentence must go. The paired-delta CSVs were produced by code that is not in `scripts/`, so they cannot be regenerated.

**Second bookkeeping problem.** `PRETRAINED_LADDER_RESULTS.md` says rungs are cumulative. In `pretrained_ladder/metadata.json` r4/r5/r6 are each *control + one group* (23/25/25 features), whereas the walk-forward r5/r6 are cumulative (27/31 features, `walk_forward/cells/r5_plus_fed-fold2022-seed42/comparison_metadata.json`). Consequently pretrained WQL for r5/r6 differs between the two runs by up to 0.0028 (2023 r6: 0.6837 vs 0.6865) while r0–r4 and r7 agree to <1e-5. Not leakage, but the two documents describe different experiments under the same rung names, and the FINETUNED doc's "Fed-tone rung" is a different feature set from the ladder's.

**(b) Permutation, pretrained (`permutation_*/importance_by_window.csv`).** Reproduced: vxn_log_chg −0.0138 [−0.0275, −0.0014] (42 features), −0.0134 (control-21), −0.0141 (same-session t0); overnight_gap −0.0073 [−0.0131, −0.0021] in control-21 but only −0.0013 (n.s.) with 42 features; gdelt_fed_avg_tone +0.00175 [+0.0002, +0.0034]. Two caveats the doc omits: (i) 42 uncorrected tests at 95% yield ~2 false positives, and three features cleared the bar — the Fed-tone result (0.25% of the loss) is exactly what multiplicity produces; the "two independent methods point at Fed" argument is weak because the ladder's rung is FOMC-classifier tone (pooled n.s.) and the permutation hit is GDELT Fed tone, different variables. (ii) The permutation tool uses per-window WQL (`baseline_wql`), the quiet-week-inflating normalisation the same doc criticises for the ladder. Re-weighting by window sum|y| gives VXN −0.017 [−0.035, −0.004], so the sign survives.

**(c) Fine-tuning gain (`walk_forward/window_losses.csv`, seed-averaged per window).** Reproduced: r0 −0.0001, r3 −0.0008 [−0.0038, +0.0023], r6 −0.0018 [−0.0056, +0.0017], r7-bw4 −0.0048 [−0.0105, +0.0007]; r7 minus matched control +0.0013 [−0.0012, +0.0038]; recipe effect +0.0010. The one significant cell (r4, 2022, −0.0063 [−0.0126, −0.0005]) is one of 28 cells, and one window (origin 2022-03-08, −0.096) carries a third of it (excluding it: −0.0044). Seeds disagree on the sign for the control rung (seed42 −0.0020, seed43 +0.0012, seed44 −0.0015). "No rung's gain excludes zero" is right; "concentrated in 2022" is a single fragile cell.

**(d) Coverage (`cells/*/validation_metrics.csv`).** Reproduced: 10–90 PT 0.760/0.792/0.755/0.800 → FT 0.787/0.812/0.781/0.818; FT > PT in 103/108 cells; 1–99 0.966 → 0.977 (88/92 cells). Mean 10–90 width grows by 8% (ratio 1.06–1.09 in every fold). Unstated: the Gaussian baseline is *better* calibrated (0.80–0.82) than the pretrained model.

**(e) "Indistinguishable" profiles (`permutation_finetuned/runs/`).** Pearson between pretrained and fine-tuned importance vectors: 0.948, 0.970, 0.963, 0.978, 0.938, 0.908 (Spearman 0.62–0.97); max |difference| 0.0015–0.0023 against top importances of 0.004–0.008; mean |difference| 0.0002–0.0006. Fair for the top features. Missed: in both 2022 cells `d_dgs2_bp` moved from misleading (−0.0020) to neutral (−0.0003), +0.0017 significant in r3 and r5 alike — the one small case of unlearning, in the hiking year. Also `mom_21d` becomes significantly misleading after FT in 2022 (−0.0016).

## 2. What the write-ups missed

1. **Fine-tuning is an unconditional widening, and its net-zero gain hides a large, significant sign flip by volatility.** Pooled r3–r6, windows split by realised mean|return| (from the ladder actuals, same windows to 3e-7): low tercile **+0.0090 [+0.0058, +0.0122]** (fine-tuning hurts), high tercile **−0.0103 [−0.0165, −0.0044]** (helps); Spearman −0.39 to −0.49 on every rung including r0 and r7. Holds within fold (r3: +0.0074 / −0.0117) and in relative terms (+2.6% / −0.65%). The 2022 "gain" is simply the high-vol fold (mean|ret| 0.0163 vs 0.0085–0.0095). Seeds agree on this window pattern (per-window gain correlation between seeds 0.53–0.74). This is the real mechanism: LoRA learns "be 8% wider", not a conditional scale, and it is the strongest fine-tuning result in the artifacts.
2. **Per horizon.** The gain sits at h1: −0.0057 (negative in all four folds, 7 of 8 rungs); h2–h5 within ±0.0013. Pretrained 10–90 coverage is worst at h1 (0.736) and FT lifts it to 0.773. The VXN permutation effect is *not* "present at every horizon": h1 −0.005 to −0.009, h2–h4 −0.017 to −0.027, h5 ≈ 0 in all three 2025H2 runs.
3. **VXN is five weeks.** Mean −0.0134 vs median −0.0023; the five worst windows (origins 2025-10-07 −0.173, 2025-08-18 −0.143, 2025-07-28 −0.137, 2026-02-17 −0.103, 2025-12-17 −0.076) are 94% of the total; mean without them −0.0009. Low-|VXN-move| tercile +0.007 (n.s.). Split by volatility surprise (5-day forward / 20-day trailing |ret|): calm weeks +0.0015, shock weeks **−0.031***; the top three are the Oct-2025 and Aug-2025 shocks (ratios 3.2, 2.1, 1.5). So "the model misreads the lagged VXN move" is not established: the data fit equally well "calm VXN context → narrower bands → punished by unforeseeable shocks; a random donor series acts as a regulariser". In the 2022 cell the effect is a third the size (−0.0040) and unrelated to surprise.
4. **Known-future flags after FT: still nothing.** FT r2−r1 −0.0006 [−0.0026, +0.0013]; `days_to_fomc` within ±0.0007 in all six FT cells; `is_fomc_day` slightly negative everywhere. The open question in the ladder doc is answered: no.
5. **Models beat the Gaussian pooled, and it is significant.** FT r3 minus Gaussian −0.0109 [−0.0185, −0.0037] (2025 −0.038*, 2024 −0.011*, 2022 +0.006 n.s.). Neither doc prints this, and it is the cleanest "skill over naive" number available.
6. **Unmentioned significant cells:** FT r3 vs r2 in 2023 +0.0037 [+0.0001, +0.0075] (adding market covariates hurts the fine-tuned 2023 model; it is in `marginal_group_effect.csv` but not discussed); PT r5 vs r4 2023 +0.0018*. Both are single cells at 95%.
7. **1–99 band:** pretrained 0.945 in 2024 (0.936–0.952 across rungs) and 0.957 in 2025 — tails too thin; FT lifts to 0.959/0.969, still under 0.98. Direction: FT 0.527 vs always-up 0.544 — below base rate, as stated.

## 3. Sanity / leakage

- `docs/results/pretrained_ladder/ladder_metrics.csv` == `models/pretrained-ladder/ladder_metrics.csv` (max diff 0). Walk-forward pretrained arm matches the ladder to <1e-5 for r0–r4, r7 (MPS vs CUDA agree); r5/r6 differ only through the feature-set mismatch above. Pretrained WQL is identical across the four arm folders of each rung/fold.
- 388 (rung, fold, arm, model) groups × exactly 50 windows; 0 duplicate keys; 0 NaN. 45 ladder files × 250 rows, 0 duplicates/NaN. Permutation files: 50 × 3 × {21, 27, 42}, 0 NaN.
- All 140 `comparison_metadata.json`: `train_end` = Dec 31 of the prior year < `validation_start`; `effective_train_batch_size` = windows × variates (176 = 8×22, 172 = 4×43); steps × windows = 4,000 for every fine-tuned cell. Reproducibility caveat: `git_dirty: true` in all 140 cells across three commits (95ec57c, d14226c, 5840a8c).
- Quirks: r7 has no 8-window arm, so `walk_forward_summary.md` shows `nan` rows; the 2022 fold has 251 validation rows, one unused.

## 4. Verdict

**Solid enough to present:** (i) zero-shot covariates do nothing, 42 of them hurt (+0.0045*); (ii) fine-tuning does not change loss on any rung, nor covariate usage (profile correlation 0.91–0.98); (iii) fine-tuning widens intervals ~8% and fixes coverage on every rung including target-only (103/108 cells); (iv) the widening helps in volatile weeks and hurts in quiet weeks (both significant) — the mechanism behind (ii) and (iii); (v) all models beat the Gaussian pooled but lose in 2022.

**Overstated:** the r0 "control helps via 2025" line (a bug); "Fed tone is the one input the model uses" (one of ~2 expected false positives, two different variables); "the model misreads the lagged VXN move" (five shock weeks; mechanism unproven); the r4-2022 gain (one of 28 cells, one window); "rungs are cumulative" for the ladder; "VXN effect at every horizon".
